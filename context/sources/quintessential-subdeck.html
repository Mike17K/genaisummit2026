03

Ideas to steal

Four places an agent could go to work. Sixteen starting points. Take one, bend it, make it yours.

This weekend's theme

“Agents are moving out of the demo and into the job.”

So the question for every idea: what is the tedious, multi-step job nobody wants to do twice — and can an agent do all of it, end to end?

Direction 1 · the work nobody wants

Agents at the office

The phone call you avoid

A voice agent that waits on hold, negotiates, and reports back with what was agreed.

The meeting that writes its own tickets

Listens in, creates tasks, assigns owners, chases them a week later.

Inbox to done

Reads the mail, drafts replies, books the meeting, files the invoice. Shows you a diff of your day.

On-call sidekick

Alert fires at 3am. It reads the logs, finds the commit, proposes the fix before you're awake.

Direction 2 · science, faster

Agents in the lab

The lab notebook that listens

Talk at the bench; get a structured protocol, a results table and tomorrow's reagent order.

Fifty papers before lunch

An agent that reads the literature and proposes the next experiment, with the page it got each claim from.

Trial matcher

A patient summary in; the clinical trials they could join out, explained in plain language.

Instrument whisperer

Watches the raw output of a lab machine, flags the odd run, drafts the report.

Direction 3 · content and digital art

Agents in the studio

Script to storyboard

A page of script in; consistent characters, shots and a rough animatic out.

The game that builds itself

Levels, rules and art generated from how you played the last round.

Campaign in a box

One brief; every format, every channel, checked against the brand guide by an agent that says no.

Visuals that listen

Live generative visuals driven by the music, the room or the speaker's voice.

Direction 4 · the physical world (hardware earns extra points)

Agents in the wild

Grandma's button

One physical button. Press it and a voice reads her letters, explains the bill, calls the pharmacy.

Point and ask

Aim a camera at a machine, a shelf, a monument. Get answers about the thing in front of you.

Bureaucracy navigator

“I need to renew X.” It finds the forms, fills what it can, tells you what to bring.

Argue with Socrates

Pitch your startup. He only answers with questions. (The ancient one. Ours is friendlier.)

How to win the weekend

What you're judged on

Usefulness, craft, and aura points

Aura points

The thing we're still talking about on the way home. Taste, nerve, a surprise.

Scope

One user.
One job, end to end.
One wow moment.

Everything that isn't on the path to the wow moment gets faked, hard-coded or cut.

Pace · 29 hours from first commit to code freeze

Spend the weekend like this

Sat 13:00DecideTeam, track, the person it's for. Kill the other ideas before lunch.

OvernightMake the wow realThen actually sleep. Tired teams ship bugs.

Field rules

Things that save teams

Demo first

Write the 3-minute script before the code. Build only what the script needs.

Use real data

A real inbox, a real PDF, a real photo. Lorem ipsum hides every interesting problem.

Let the agents build it

Use coding agents for the scaffolding. Save your brain for the idea.

Show the work

Stream steps, tool calls, sources. Watching an agent do the job is half the magic.

Have a plan B

Wi-fi dies, APIs rate-limit. Cache a good run. The demo video is your insurance.

Ask us

Stuck for 20 minutes? Come and find Socrates or me. That's why we're here.
