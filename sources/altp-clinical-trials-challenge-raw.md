# Athens LifeHack – The Clinical Trials Challenge
**A call for AI solutions that make clinical trials faster, smarter and more patient-centric.**

*Exact unedited text from the organizer's brief (`ALTP_Clinical_Trials_Challenge_draft (3).docx`).*

---

Clinical trials are the bridge between scientific discovery and patient benefit, and they are also the most expensive and time-consuming phase of bringing a new medicine to market. Trials are delayed by protocols that are difficult to execute, by slow and unrepresentative recruitment, by participants who drop out because taking part is too demanding, and by monitoring processes that detect problems only after they have affected the study. Many promising treatments are delayed or abandoned not because they do not work, but because the trials designed to test them could not be run efficiently.

Artificial intelligence offers a real opportunity to change this, from the design of the protocol to the last patient visit. We are inviting teams to demonstrate how AI can address a specific, well-defined bottleneck in clinical development, and how their solution could be adopted by sponsors, CROs, hospitals and clinical sites.

---

## Challenge topics

### 1. Protocol Design
The protocol is the blueprint of every clinical trial. Decisions made at this stage determine everything that follows: cost, timelines, patient burden, data quality and, in the end, whether the trial can answer its scientific question. Yet protocols are often built from precedent and expert intuition rather than evidence. The result is trials with overly restrictive eligibility criteria, endpoints that are hard to measure, visit schedules that burden patients and sites, and frequent amendments that add months and significant cost mid-study.

We are looking for AI-driven solutions that help sponsors and investigators design protocols that are scientifically robust, operationally feasible and patient-centric from day one.

**In scope:**
* Simulating the impact of eligibility criteria on the size of the available patient pool and on trial outcomes
* Endpoint selection and optimisation, including digital and patient-reported endpoints
* Predicting protocol complexity, amendment risk and site burden before the study starts
* Mining historical trial data (registries, publications, regulatory documents) to benchmark design choices
* Adaptive, Bayesian and synthetic or external control arm designs
* Generative AI tools that help draft, review and check protocols for consistency and regulatory alignment

### 2. Patient Selection and Recruitment
Recruitment is the most common cause of clinical trial delays. Most trials fail to enroll on time, and many sites enroll few or no patients at all. At the same time, eligible patients often never learn that a relevant trial exists, and trial populations frequently fail to reflect the diversity of the people who will eventually use the treatment. Matching patients to trials still depends heavily on manual chart review and the personal networks of investigators.

We are looking for AI solutions that connect the right patients with the right trials efficiently, fairly and in compliance with privacy and data protection requirements.

**In scope:**
* Automated patient-to-trial matching using electronic health records, structured data and clinical notes (NLP)
* Prediction of site performance and data-driven site selection and feasibility assessment
* Identifying under-represented populations and supporting more diverse and inclusive enrolment
* Patient-facing tools that make trials easier to find and eligibility easier to understand
* Recruitment forecasting and real-time enrolment optimization
* Privacy-preserving approaches, such as federated learning, for working across hospitals and data sources

### 3. Patient Experience
Clinical trials measure whether a treatment works, but they are far less effective at capturing how patients actually feel while taking it. Symptoms, side effects, fatigue, mood, sleep, and the impact on daily life and quality of life are usually recorded through periodic questionnaires completed at site visits. These snapshots miss what happens between visits, depend on recall, and rarely capture the patient's own words. As a result, important information about tolerability and benefit as experienced by patients is lost, even though it increasingly matters to regulators, payers, clinicians and patients themselves.

We are looking for AI solutions that capture, measure and interpret how patients experience a treatment, continuously, reliably and in a way that can feed into trial decisions and evidence.

**In scope:**
* Smarter collection of patient-reported outcomes (PROs), including adaptive and personalized questionnaires
* Analysis of free text, voice and conversational input to capture symptoms, side effects and wellbeing in the patient's own words
* Use of wearables and digital biomarkers (e.g. sleep, activity, heart rate) to assess how patients feel between visits
* Early detection of emerging side effects, declining tolerability or worsening quality of life
* Turning patient experience data into robust, interpretable endpoints that can support regulatory and clinical decisions
* Multilingual and accessible tools so that all patients, including elderly or vulnerable participants, can express how they feel

### 4. Clinical Trial Monitoring
Clinical trials generate vast amounts of data across sites, systems and countries. Ensuring that this data is accurate, that patients are safe and that the protocol is followed has traditionally relied on manual source data verification and periodic site visits. This approach is costly and slow, and it often detects problems only after they have affected the study. Regulators increasingly encourage risk-based quality management, but many organizations lack the tools to put it into practice.

We are looking for AI solutions that enable continuous, risk-based monitoring and help teams detect and act on issues before they compromise patient safety or data integrity.

**In scope:**
* Risk-based and centralized monitoring, including key risk indicators and site risk scoring
* Anomaly detection for data quality issues, protocol deviations and potential misconduct or fraud
* Early safety signal detection and support for pharmacovigilance and adverse event reporting
* Automated data cleaning, query management and reconciliation across data sources
* Predictive analytics for trial performance, timelines and operational bottlenecks
* Explainable AI dashboards that support monitors, data managers and safety committees
